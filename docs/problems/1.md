## Problem Statement

Consider the stochastic subgradient method applied to a locally Lipschitz,
possibly nonconvex function $f: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$:

$$
x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k g_k,
\qquad
g_k \in \partial f(x_k),
$$

where $\partial f$ denotes the Clarke subdifferential and
$\mathbb{E}[g_k \mid x_k] \in \partial f(x_k)$.

Under what explicit assumptions on:

- the function class (e.g., weak convexity, definability, stratification),
- the stochastic model (noise, martingale-difference structure),
- and the stepsizes $(\eta_k)$,

can one guarantee that the iterates converge *only to local minimizers*,
rather than to non-minimizing stationary points (e.g., nonsmooth saddles),
with almost-sure or high-probability guarantees?

---

## § Significance & Implications

Many modern machine learning models (ReLU networks, robust losses, composite
regularized objectives) are:

- nonsmooth,
- nonconvex,
- locally Lipschitz,
- often semialgebraic or definable.

The stochastic subgradient method (SSGM) is a canonical algorithm for such
problems. Classical theory guarantees convergence to stationary points under
convexity, but in nonconvex nonsmooth settings the geometry of stationary
points is far more delicate.

A theory showing that SSGM converges *only to local minimizers*
under realistic geometric conditions would:

- clarify why nonsmooth training often succeeds in practice,
- provide structural guidance for model design,
- bridge smooth strict-saddle theory with nonsmooth geometry.

## § Known Partial Results

### 1. Convergence to Stationary Points on Tame Landscapes

For locally Lipschitz functions that are **semialgebraic / definable**
("tame"), the stochastic subgradient method converges (in an almost-sure
sense) to the set of **Clarke stationary points** under standard stochastic
approximation stepsizes.

This ensures that limit points satisfy

$$
0 \in \partial f(x^\star).
$$

However, this does *not* exclude convergence to non-minimizing stationary
points.

**Reference:**  
Davis, Drusvyatskiy, Kakade, Lee (2019).  
*Stochastic subgradient method converges on tame functions.*

---

### 2. Rates for Weakly Convex Functions

For **weakly convex** functions, stochastic (proximal) subgradient methods
achieve quantitative convergence to approximate stationary points.

Using the Moreau envelope $f_\lambda$, one obtains

$$
\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f_\lambda(x_k)\|^2 = O(k^{-1/2}),
$$

implying $O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ complexity for
$\varepsilon$-stationarity.

These are rates toward first-order stationarity,
not toward local minimality.

**Reference:**  
Davis, Drusvyatskiy (2018).  
*Stochastic subgradient method converges at rate $O(k^{-1/4})$ on weakly convex functions.*

---

### 3. Active Strict Saddles (Nonsmooth Strict-Saddle Geometry)

To generalize strict-saddle theory to nonsmooth settings, the notion of
**active strict saddles** was introduced.

A point is an active strict saddle if:

- it lies on a smooth stratum where the function behaves smoothly,
- and exhibits a direction of negative curvature along that active manifold,
- while off the manifold the subgradient norm is bounded away from zero.

Under this geometry:

- non-minimizing stationary points possess unstable directions,
- making them candidates for avoidance by stochastic dynamics.

**Reference:**  
Davis, Drusvyatskiy (2021).  
*Active strict saddles in nonsmooth optimization.*

---

### 4. Avoidance of Active Strict Saddles by Stochastic Subgradient Descent

Recent results show that stochastic subgradient descent
**does not converge to active strict saddles**
under suitable geometric and stochastic assumptions, including:

- weak convexity,
- stratification regularity,
- an angle condition,
- nondegenerate stochastic noise.

Consequently, if all non-minimizing stationary points are active strict saddles,
then stochastic subgradient descent converges only to local minimizers
(generically, in definable weakly convex classes).

**Reference:**  
Bianchi, Hachem, Schechtman (2021).  
*Stochastic Subgradient Descent Escapes Active Strict Saddles on Weakly Convex Functions.*

---

## § Remaining Gaps

Despite substantial progress, several questions remain open:

- Fully explicit, minimal geometric conditions guaranteeing
  convergence exclusively to local minima.
- Finite-time high-probability bounds (beyond asymptotic almost-sure results).
- Extension beyond weak convexity.
- Quantitative rates to local minimizers (not merely stationarity).
- Characterization of degenerate nonsmooth critical points.
