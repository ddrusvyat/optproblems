## Problem Statement

Consider gradient-based methods for minimizing a smooth nonconvex function
$f:\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$:

$$
x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k \nabla f(x_k).
$$

Under what explicit assumptions on $f$, initialization, and stepsizes $(\eta_k)$
can one guarantee that iterates converge to local minima rather than saddle points
or non-minimizing stationary points, with quantitative rates or high-probability
guarantees?

## § Significance & Implications

This question is central to modern optimization and machine learning: many training
objectives are nonconvex, while practical algorithms often perform well despite
limited theory. Strong local-minimum convergence guarantees would improve algorithm
design, stopping criteria, and reliability claims.

## § Known Partial Results

- Strict-saddle frameworks show avoidance of unstable saddles under perturbations.
- Gradient-flow and KL-type analyses often establish convergence to critical points.
- For special structures (e.g., Morse-type landscapes), stronger local guarantees are available.
