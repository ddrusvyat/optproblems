Convergence to Local Minima

Problem 1 · Nonconvex Optimization · Optimization Theory

§ Problem Statement

Consider gradient-based methods for minimizing a smooth nonconvex function $f:\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}$:

$$ x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta_k \nabla f(x_k). $$

Under what explicit assumptions on $f$, initialization, and stepsizes $(\eta_k)$ can one guarantee that iterates converge to local minima rather than saddle points or non-minimizing stationary points, with quantitative rates or high-probability guarantees?

Copy-pastable prompt

Study the open problem "Convergence to local minima" for nonconvex optimization. Setup: - Iteration: x_{k+1} = x_k - eta_k grad f(x_k), f: R^d -> R smooth and nonconvex. - Current global assumptions often guarantee only convergence to critical points. Open question: - Find practical, verifiable assumptions under which trajectories converge to local minima. - Characterize when strict saddles are avoided (deterministically or with random perturbations). - Provide finite-time and asymptotic rates that scale with dimension and conditioning. - Clarify how noise, stochastic gradients, and momentum change the guarantees. Deliverables: - Theorem-level conditions. - Examples/counterexamples. - References and comparison with known strict-saddle and Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz results.

§ Significance & Implications

This question is central to modern optimization and machine learning: many training objectives are nonconvex, while practical algorithms often perform well despite limited theory. Strong local-minimum convergence guarantees would improve algorithm design, stopping criteria, and reliability claims.

§ Known Partial Results

  • Strict-saddle frameworks show avoidance of unstable saddles under perturbations.
  • Gradient-flow and KL-type analyses often establish convergence to critical points.
  • For special structures (e.g., Morse-type landscapes), stronger local guarantees are available.
